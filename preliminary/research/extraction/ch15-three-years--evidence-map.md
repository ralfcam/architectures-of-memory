### CH15-C01 / C15-01 — Extraordinary change by age three

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | The first three years involve major reorganization across biological, cognitive, emotional, and social systems. Age three is a waypoint, not a finished person. |
| Label | `interpretive-only` this pass. The empirical pieces already live on Part I–II cards. |
| Grade | High as description of accumulated change; Low as a single quantitative endpoint. |

### CH15-C03 — Rank-order continuity is moderate, not destiny

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Early individual differences show modest rank-order stability. Roberts & DelVecchio (2000) find personality/temperament consistency is an increasing function of age; the childhood bin is far from 1.0. Continuity is not adult personality written at 36 months. |
| Sources | Roberts & DelVecchio 2000 *Psychological Bulletin* DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.126.1.3. |
| Label | `promote` — card `evidence-cards/rank-order-continuity-not-destiny.md` |
| Grade | Moderate-to-High for modest early rank-order consistency as a meta-analytic pattern; Low as an individual adult-personality forecast. |

### CH15-C04 — Fadeout of early test-score gains

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Early cognitive/test-score advantages often attenuate; some intensive early-education RCTs still show later-life effects. Fadeout is not proof of null long-run impact, and persistence is not a license for any program at scale. |
| Label | **Reconcile only** — `preschool-early-education-rct.md`. |
| Grade | Moderate (unchanged). |

### CH15-C05 — Ordinary resilience

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Resilience commonly arises from ordinary adaptive systems (relationships, regulation, competence, community resources), not rare invulnerability. Adversity is not harmless. |
| Sources | Masten 2001 *American Psychologist* DOI 10.1037/0003-066X.56.3.227. |
| Label | `promote` — card `evidence-cards/ordinary-resilience-adaptive-systems.md` |
| Grade | High that resilience processes exist and are often ordinary; Moderate for any single mechanism or rate. |

### CH15-C06 — Plasticity after three

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Environments can still move outcomes after age three. BEIP family placement after severe deprivation is the upper-bound demonstration, not ordinary catch-up. |
| Label | **Reconcile only** — `severe-institutional-deprivation.md`. |
| Grade | High danger / Moderate-to-High placement; Very Low that effect sizes describe ordinary three-year-olds. |

### CH15-C07 — Childhood self-control as a population gradient

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | In the Dunedin cohort, a childhood self-control gradient predicts later health, wealth, and public-safety outcomes; children whose self-control improved did better than their early standing implied. This is not a marshmallow-test replication and not an individual sentence at age three. |
| Sources | Moffitt et al. 2011 *PNAS* DOI 10.1073/pnas.1010076108. |
| Label | `promote` — card `evidence-cards/childhood-self-control-gradient.md` |
| Grade | Moderate population gradient; Low individual forecast. Watts delay card reconciled, not rewritten. |

## 3. Prohibited inferences

- Age three ≠ destiny
- Continuity ≠ individual forecast
- Dunedin gradient ≠ marshmallow replication
- Resilience ≠ adversity is harmless
- Plasticity ≠ everything reversible
- BEIP ≠ ordinary catch-up
- Fadeout ≠ “early programs do nothing”
