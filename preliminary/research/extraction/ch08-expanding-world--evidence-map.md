## 2. Candidate claims

Labels: `promote` · `needs-source-audit` · `interpretive-only` · `exclude`.

### CH08-C01 — Infants can form attachment relationships with more than one caregiver

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Infants can organize relationship-specific expectations with more than one caregiver; network configuration is a better object than a single score. Adding caregivers is not automatically better. |
| Sources | Dagan & Sagi-Schwartz 2021 *NDCAD* DOI 10.1002/cad.20446 PMID 34936190; Dagan & Sagi-Schwartz 2018 *CDP* DOI 10.1111/cdep.12272. |
| Label | `promote` — card `evidence-cards/infant-multiple-caregiver-attachments.md` |
| Grade | Moderate that multiple attachments occur; Low that network size is a dose. |

### CH08-C02 — Social referencing under uncertainty

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Toward the end of the first year, infants can use a familiar adult’s emotional signal to regulate approach or withdrawal in an ambiguous situation. This is a social-information use, not a verdict on attachment security. |
| Sources | Sorce, Emde, Campos & Klinnert 1985 *Developmental Psychology* DOI 10.1037/0012-1649.21.1.195; Walden & Ogan 1988 *Child Development* DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8624.1988.tb01492.x. |
| Label | `promote` — card `evidence-cards/infant-social-referencing.md` |
| Grade | High for the classic visual-cliff / ambiguous-toy laboratory phenomenon; Low that referencing frequency measures parental worth or attachment category. |

### CH08-C03 — Stranger wariness is not insecure attachment

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Social selectivity and wariness of unfamiliar people become more visible across late infancy; they are expected developmental phenomena in many contexts, not a classification of attachment insecurity. |
| Sources | Named in C08-P1 timeline; not separately carded this pass. |
| Label | `interpretive-only` (prohibition lives on the social-referencing and multiple-caregiver cards). |
| Grade | Moderate as description; Very Low as an attachment diagnosis. |

### CH08-C04 — Peer interaction as a less accommodating partner

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Across the second and third years, another toddler is a less motivated, less skilled partner than a caregiver. Reciprocal coordination, conflict over objects, and repair become possible; more peer exposure is not automatically better. |
| Sources | Brownell, Ramani & Zerwas 2006 *Child Development* DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8624.2006.00904.x. |
| Label | `promote` — card `evidence-cards/toddler-peer-interaction.md` |
| Grade | Moderate that peer contexts create distinctive coordination demands; Low that peer hours cause later prosocial character. |

### CH08-C05 — Group-care quality versus quantity

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Attendance is not a unitary exposure. Process quality (warm, language-rich interaction) is more consistently related to early cognitive/language outcomes than hours alone; family characteristics often predict more. Hours–externalizing links are small and not a law. |
| Sources | NICHD SECCYD booklet `nichd-seccyd-2006`; Vandell 2006 / 2010; Belsky 2007. |
| Label | **Reconcile only** — existing `early-childcare-quality.md`. Do not re-extract. |
| Grade | Moderate (unchanged). |

## 3. Prohibited inferences

- Stranger anxiety ≠ insecure attachment
- Group care ≠ deprivation
- More caregivers ≠ better development
- Peer exposure ≠ automatic social competence
- Childcare attendance ≠ harm or benefit as a moral category
- A larger network is not a prescription
