# Paternal Age and De Novo Mutation Rate

> Domain: preconception
> Last updated: 2026-09-16
> Status: Source-audited first pass (CH01-C06)

## What We Know

Sperm production involves continual cell division. In whole-genome sequences of parent–offspring trios, Kong et al. (2012) showed that de novo mutations in offspring increase with father’s age, on the order of two additional mutations per year of paternal age.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: High for that mutational relationship as a genetic observation. **Low** that paternal age, in ordinary ranges, determines a child’s psychiatric or cognitive future.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Kong et al. (2012), *Nature*: Icelandic trio sequencing. The age association is a property of germline mutation, not a randomized trial of delayed fatherhood. Advanced paternal age has been associated with selected perinatal and neurodevelopmental outcomes in later observational reviews; those associations are generally modest and entangled with socioeconomic conditions, reproductive selection, assortative mating, and inherited liability.

## Effect Size and Persistence

The defensible unit is additional de novo mutations per year of paternal age, not IQ points or diagnostic risk for a particular child. Absolute risk in ordinary age ranges is the quantity that interpretation requires and that popular summaries often omit.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **n/a** for the mutation-rate claim (sequence observation). Observational outcome associations are a different claim and are not classified as robust here.

## What Remains Uncertain

How much of later neurodevelopmental association, if any, is carried by the mutational increment versus selection and shared family factors.

## What This Does Not Mean

Does not mean “have children early or risk damaging them.” Does not license ranking families by the age at which they conceived. A biologically true mutational finding can still support a wrong causal story about a child’s future.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Reproductive counseling that reports absolute risks in context. Fertility instructions and character forecasts do not follow.
