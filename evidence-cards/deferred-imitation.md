# Deferred Imitation After a Delay

> Domain: early learning
> Last updated: 2026-09-16
> Status: Source-audited first pass (CH06 deferred imitation)

## What We Know

Infants can encode a modeled action and reproduce it later without having practiced it in the interval. Meltzoff (1988), *Developmental Psychology*: 14-month-olds reproduced novel object acts after a **one-week** delay. The design compares infants who saw the target act with controls who saw the object or an unrelated act, which is stronger identification than “babies copy.”

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: High that deferred imitation of novel object acts occurs at 14 months after a one-week delay in this design. **Low** that the same fact licenses neonatal imitation (a live controversy, not this card), unlimited delay, copying of every demonstrated act, or a home curriculum of demonstrations.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Experimental modeling with delay and control conditions. Later work varies delay, number of acts, and context match. Context specificity and motor opportunity still constrain performance, as in other infant memory paradigms (`infant-contingency-learning.md`).

## Effect Size and Persistence

The unit is reproduction of a specified act after a stated delay, not a general “imitation IQ.” A missed demonstration is not a missed mind.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **n/a**. Arousal, interest in the object, and whether the infant can perform the motor act all affect scoring. Laboratory novelty is not everyday cultural learning, though it is a route into it.

## What Remains Uncertain

How deferred imitation relates to later pedagogical learning; boundary conditions on delay and context; the status of neonatal imitation, which this card does not carry.

## What This Does Not Mean

Does not mean newborns imitate facial gestures as a settled fact. Does not mean infants copy blindly. Does not mean parents must demonstrate more actions. Does not mean a child who does not reproduce an act failed to remember it (performance versus memory).

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Ordinary opportunities to watch people act on objects are consistent with the finding. Imitation drills do not follow.
