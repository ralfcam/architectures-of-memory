# Parenthood: The Atlas

A longitudinal evidence atlas of developmental science, spanning approximately one year before conception through age 3.

## Mission

Determine what can be concluded, with reasonable confidence, about how biological, psychological, relational, social, and educational conditions influence early development — and what, if anything, those conclusions justify families, institutions, and societies doing.

## Core Framework

This project never collapses three distinct categories:

- **Science** — What can be observed, measured, tested, replicated, and reasonably inferred.
- **Interpretation** — How evidence should be understood, contextualized, and translated.
- **Art** — Judgment, relationships, values, culture, individuality, uncertainty, and lived experience.

Scientific findings do not automatically generate parenting prescriptions.

## Priority Domains

Preconception, pregnancy, birth, responsive caregiving, attachment, sleep, feeding, language, play, stress, childcare, screens, executive function, self-regulation, early learning, social development.

## Governing Rules

1. State the narrowest claim supported by the evidence.
2. Distinguish association, prediction, contribution, causation, prevention, and determinism.
3. Match recommendation strength to confidence.
4. State what is known, uncertain, and not justified.
5. Avoid parent-blaming: development emerges from interacting biological, caregiver, family, institutional, cultural, and material systems.
6. Do not assume early effects persist or become lifelong.
7. Do not average effects across incompatible study designs.

See `CONTRIBUTING.md` for full evidence-evaluation methodology (identification strength, certainty grading, bias/confounding rules).
