# Chapter 01 — The First Environment (Seed Outline)

## 1. Phenomenon
A pregnant person talks to their unborn baby. After birth, the newborn turns toward that same voice.

## 2. Puzzle
How does a fetus learn before birth? What can be learned, and what is innate?

## 3. Case
A mother reads aloud during pregnancy. Her newborn, hours after birth, shows a preference for that story over an unfamiliar one.

## 4. Question
What does prenatal exposure establish? Is it learning, or is it a bias in perception?

## 5. Competing Explanations
- **Prenatal learning**: The fetus encodes auditory patterns.
- **Perceptual tuning**: Exposure biases attention, not memory.
- **Genetic confounding**: Maternal traits that produce prenatal exposure also produce postnatal behavior.

## 6. Mechanism
- Auditory system development in utero.
- Habituation and memory mechanisms.
- Postnatal reinforcement loops (parent continues the same behavior).

## 7. Evidence
- Observational studies of prenatal exposure and postnatal preference.
- Genetically informed designs (sibling comparisons) that attenuate some effects.
- Certainty grade: **Moderate** for perceptual tuning; **Low** for long-term learning effects.

## 8. Boundary
- Evidence does not establish that prenatal exposure causes lasting cognitive advantages.
- Most effects are short-lived and interact heavily with postnatal environment.

## 9. System
- Connects to later language development, attachment, and regulatory capacity.
- Illustrates the Atlas Principle: mapping boundaries between prenatal and postnatal systems.

## 10. Return
The newborn has not changed. The reader now sees the womb not as a sealed chamber but as the first environment in a continuous developmental stream.
