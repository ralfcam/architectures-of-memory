# Chapter 13 — The Ecology of Development

A parent is told that development is “all about relationships.” The same week the rent is late, the shift changes, and the only childcare slot is across a highway. The child is the same child. The conditions are not.

The scene is ordinary. It is also easy to over-read.

If parents were the residual explanation, the late rent would be a motivation problem. If place were destiny, the highway would already have written the child’s scores. Neither reading fits what can be shown.

This chapter asks a narrower question than “do parents still matter?” It asks what kind of ecology a 0–3 life actually sits in, and what kinds of claims that ecology can support.

Chapter 8 described the expanding social world. Chapter 14 will ask what interventions can do. This chapter describes the material and institutional surround.

**What happens when parenting is treated as a proximal process inside systems?**

## The puzzle

Development emerges through proximal relationships inside broader material, institutional, and cultural contexts. Parenting is enabled and constrained by conditions families do not individually control. Poverty is associated with disadvantage through multiple pathways. A defined cash transfer can change an infant brain-activity measure without producing preregistered age-four cognitive gains. A neighborhood move can change later earnings depending on the child’s age at the move — and still not be a toddler-development law.

The naive picture is parental will. The equally naive picture is place as fate. What we meet instead is interacting processes: money, housing, hours, services, and relationships, none of which is a complete explanation.

Poverty is not evidence of low parental commitment. A cash-transfer null does not show that material conditions have no effects. Moving is not universally beneficial. Those are not polite disclaimers. They are part of the science.

## Competing explanations of the opening scene

Before resolving the late rent, three explanations need to sit in view:

- **Parents only.** Outcomes are a residual of caregiving quality; ecology is background noise.
- **Place as fate.** Neighborhood or income determines the child.
- **Proximal process inside constraints.** Relationships do the daily work; income, housing, and institutions shape what that work can be.

The first erases structure. The second is too complete. The third is the claim the evidence can carry.

## Ecology is not a competing residual

**What is ecology, if not a competing residual of parenting?**

Ecology, in this chapter, is the set of material, institutional, and cultural conditions that make proximal processes possible, costly, or interrupted. Bioecological and transactional pictures treat those proximal processes — the daily exchanges of care, language, and regulation — as the engines, nested in settings families did not invent. WHO’s nurturing-care framework, already catalogued on the co-regulation card, is architecture rather than a purity test. **Grade: Moderate-to-High** as a framework. **Grade: Low-to-Moderate** for any single outer-level factor taken as a law.

Parents are not irrelevant. They are not omnipotent. Race or class is not a biological explanation when structural exposure may be doing the work. Trajectories remain probabilistic and revisable.

A late rent can change sleep, meals, attention, and which adult is in the room. Those changes can look like “parenting quality” on a coding scheme. They are not thereby a motivation problem. The co-regulation card (`infant-caregiver-coregulation.md`) already refused to treat nurturing care as a purity test. Reconcile only. Sleep and attachment-intervention theory are not restated.

Return to the opening week. The shift changes. The only childcare slot is across a highway. The parent who arrives late, who has less play left in the evening, who is coded as less sensitive on a twenty-minute tape, may be the same parent who was more available last month. Ecology can masquerade as a residual of character. That is why this chapter exists.

Prenatal stress associations that attenuate under genetically informed designs already warn against treating family-level correlations as parenting effects. Adjacent: `prenatal-maternal-stress-anxiety.md`. Not rewritten. A household correlation between hardship and child scores can mix genes, selection, material deprivation, and the relationship itself. Genetically informed attenuation is a reason to keep verbs modest. It is not a claim that hardship is imaginary. It is a claim that the pathway is not automatically “parenting quality.”

Culture organizes which supports count as family, which institutions are trusted, and which hardships are named. Difference is not deficit. A kin-rich household facing the same rent is not a failed nuclear model. A nuclear household facing the same rent is not empty of ecology. Super & Harkness’s developmental-niche idea remains interpretive architecture here, as in Chapter 8: settings, customs, and caregiver psychology jointly organize experience. They do not pick a single class position as the developmental program.

## Income is not a guaranteed cognitive intervention

Low income is consistently associated with developmental and health disadvantage. That association mixes material deprivation, stress, housing, nutrition, service access, and selection. Baby’s First Years randomized mothers to a larger versus smaller monthly cash transfer. Troller-Renfree and colleagues reported greater mid-to-high-frequency infant EEG power in the high-cash group. Noble and colleagues reported no statistically significant differences on preregistered language, executive-function, social-emotional, and related developmental outcomes at age four.

**Grade: Moderate** for a socioeconomic gradient and for income’s contribution to material opportunity. **Grade: Low-to-Moderate** that unconditional cash *alone* improves early tested cognitive development. The evidence card is `income-cash-transfer-early-development.md`.

Report the infant EEG effect separately from the age-four nulls. Do not infer that a small early neural difference is a durable developmental benefit, or that a null result means poverty is harmless. An EEG difference is not a personality, not an IQ, and not a sensational neural proof. Cash is not a substitute for housing, health care, or childcare quality. Effects may depend on transfer size, duration, baseline deprivation, local prices, and other services.

The design is unusually strong for this catalog: a defined transfer, random assignment, preregistered later outcomes. That is why the age-four nulls matter. They do not cancel the gradient. They constrain the claim that cash *as such*, at this dose, in this window, is a cognitive intervention. A neural difference in infancy is a finding. It is not a school-readiness guarantee. The chapter’s job is to hold both results without collapsing them into a slogan.

A slogan would say either “cash changes the brain, therefore cash is enough” or “age four was null, therefore money does not matter.” Neither is the identification. The gradient remains. The experiment remains. The later cognitive battery did not move at the preregistered threshold. Families still cannot eat a null result. Researchers still cannot promote a neural difference into a developmental law.

Inequality distributes exposures. A gradient is not a law that every low-income child will score lower, or that every higher-income child is buffered. Cumulative advantage is probabilistic, not destiny. Those claims are interpretive in this pass. Policy effects belong to specific designs. Chapter 14 will ask what programs can do. This chapter asks what conditions those programs sit inside.

## Neighborhood moves have age-dependent later effects

Housing is a bundle: stability, crowding, affordability, safety, toxins, mobility, and neighborhood opportunity. Chetty, Hendren and Katz reanalyzed Moving to Opportunity: children who moved to lower-poverty neighborhoods *before age 13* had higher later college attendance and adult earnings; moves in adolescence produced smaller or slightly negative effects. Earlier MTO reports found few detectable effects on contemporaneous achievement — near-term scores and long-run economic outcomes can diverge.

**Grade: Moderate** for causal effects of this housing-mobility intervention on later socioeconomic outcomes, with age-at-move heterogeneity. **Grade: Low-to-Moderate** for direct effects on early (0–3) developmental measures. **Grade: Low** that every neighborhood characteristic independently affects every toddler outcome. The evidence card is `neighborhood-mobility-age-dependent.md`.

The headline later-earnings gain is not a toddler test-score effect. Moving is not universally beneficial. Place does not determine destiny. Families cannot individually solve hazardous housing and segregation. A later earnings gain is not proof that toddlers in higher-poverty neighborhoods lack parental care. Earlier MTO reports that found little on contemporaneous achievement are not a contradiction of Chetty’s later-earnings reanalysis so much as a warning: the outcome and the age window change the claim.

The highway in the opening scene is a bundle, not a score. Distance to care, traffic, air, time, and who can pick the child up are not interchangeable variables. A move that shortens a commute can change a relationship’s daily capacity without changing a neighborhood’s poverty rate. A move that changes poverty rate can leave the same commute. MTO is one experiment, not a law of place. It is also not a 0–3 developmental battery. Children who moved before age 13 include children far older than this book’s window. Treat the age-at-move finding as Moderate for later socioeconomic outcomes, not as a toddler cognition card.

Crowding, toxins, instability, and affordability can matter through different pathways — sleep, illness, attention, moves that break caregiver continuity. Those pathways are plausible. They are not each independently identified here. **Grade: Low** that every neighborhood characteristic independently affects every toddler outcome. That grade is already on the card. Keep it.

BEIP remains severe deprivation, not ordinary housing mobility. `severe-institutional-deprivation.md` is reconciled, not rewritten. Effect sizes from profound psychosocial deprivation do not describe a late rent or a highway. Childcare quality remains a different exposure. Group care is not an institution in the BEIP sense. Chapter 8 already refused that collapse. This chapter repeats the refusal because ecology writing is where the collapse returns.

## Employment, leave, food, and services stay thinner here

Employment and leave are heterogeneous packages (hours, predictability, pay, eligibility). Child-development effects are mixed. A night shift and a salaried leave are not one exposure. **Grade: Low-to-Moderate.** `needs-source-audit`. Food security is similarly held. They are not v0.2 gates. They are v0.3 candidates alongside remaining unstarted domains.

The shift in the opening scene is the employment object in miniature. Unpredictable hours can break the continuity Chapter 8 already distinguished from institutional deprivation. Predictable hours can still leave a parent too depleted for the evening’s co-regulation. Those are plausible pathways. They are not a registered 0–3 employment card in this pass. Eligibility rules — who can take leave, for how long, at what wage replacement — are themselves ecological. They are not a parenting residual.

Childcare systems return as ecology rather than as a second Chapter 8. Process quality is more consistently related to early cognitive and language outcomes than hours; family characteristics often predict more. Hours–externalizing links are small and not a law. Group care is not deprivation. **Reconcile only** — `early-childcare-quality.md`. Preschool RCTs remain `preschool-early-education-rct.md`. Neither is “ecology causes IQ.” Chapter 8 already separated attendance from experience. This chapter notes that the slot across the highway is an institution, not a parenting residual. The highway is also time: commuting is unpaid work that comes out of the same day as play, meals, and recovery from a tantrum.

Services — health, early intervention, income support, housing — are not a single program. A family’s access is itself an ecological fact. Policy effects belong to the designs that produced them. A successful indicated parent-training package (Chapter 14) does not show that typical hardship is a skill deficit.

The system is nested whether or not one uses Bronfenbrenner’s diagram. A relationship (Chapter 4) does daily work. A network (Chapter 8) shares that work. A rule (Chapter 10) has to be usable in a tired evening. A wait (Chapter 11) is shorter when the adult is still in the room. A block (Chapter 12) needs a room. Money, housing, hours, and services shape all of those without replacing them. The mistake is to treat any one layer as the residual of another.

Food security, employment, and leave remain held because they are heterogeneous packages, not because they are unimportant. A night shift that removes the familiar adult from bedtime is a different exposure from a salaried parental leave. A missed meal is not a parenting style. Naming those objects without registered 0–3 identifications is the honest limit of this pass.

## What this chapter does not tell us

It does not tell us that parents are irrelevant, or that poverty is low parental commitment.

It does not tell us that a cash-transfer null means material conditions have no effects.

It does not tell us that an infant EEG difference is a durable cognitive benefit.

It does not tell us that neighborhood association is every child’s mechanism, or that moving is always beneficial.

It does not tell us that employment is a unitary exposure, or that race or class is a biological explanation.

It does not tell us that BEIP effect sizes describe ordinary ecology, or that childcare is deprivation.

It does not tell us that Chapter 14’s intervention architecture is already this identification.

It does not tell us that commuting time is a residual of motivation, or that a coded “less sensitive” evening tape is a different parent from last month.

## Science, Interpretation, Art

**Science** here is the Moderate socioeconomic gradient; the Low-to-Moderate status of cash as a cognitive intervention; and the Moderate later-earnings effect of a particular mobility experiment, with age-at-move heterogeneity. Toddler test-score laws and parental-will residuals remain Low. Employment, leave, and food security remain Low-to-Moderate and unregistered as dedicated cards.

**Interpretation** is keeping a late rent from becoming a story about the kind of parent in the room. Match verbs to grades: *shows* is rarely the right verb for ecology; *suggests* and *is associated with* do more of the work. Holding the infant EEG and the age-four nulls in the same paragraph is an interpretive discipline, not a hedge.

**Art** is deciding what a family can reasonably do this month, and what must be asked of institutions instead. The art is not to pretend the highway is a parenting problem.

## Return

The child in the apartment has not become a different child because we now have a map. The map has changed. What looked like a motivation problem is now a week of rent, shifts, and a slot across a highway — conditions a relationship still has to work inside.

A relationship still does the daily work. It does that work inside rent, shifts, rooms, and rules the family did not invent. Possibility remains. So does constraint. The late rent was never a motivation problem. The highway was never a score. Both were conditions under which a relationship still had to try. The map does not pay the rent. It changes what the late week can be taken to mean. The parent who was already trying is still the proximal process. The week is still the surround.

The telephone-block in Chapter 12 needed materials and a room. This chapter names the conditions that make the room possible. Chapter 8 described the expanding social world. This chapter describes the surround. What actually works, as a question about interventions, is the next territory. It is not a verdict on the parent who was already trying.
