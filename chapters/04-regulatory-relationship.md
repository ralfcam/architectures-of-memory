# Chapter 04 — The Regulatory Relationship (Seed Outline)

Working TOC Chapter 4. Relocated from `chapters/02-regulatory-relationship.md` so the filename matches the 15-chapter sequence. This remains a 10-step sketch, not a promoted chapter.

## 1. Phenomenon
A baby cries. A parent picks them up. The crying stops.

## 2. Puzzle
Is this "spoiling," or is it the birth of self-regulation?

## 3. Case
A 6-month-old wakes at night. One parent responds immediately; another waits. The infant's crying pattern diverges over weeks.

## 4. Question
How does co-regulation become self-regulation? Where is the boundary between support and dependence?

## 5. Competing Explanations
- **Attachment-security model**: Responsive care builds a secure base, enabling later autonomy.
- **Behavioral model**: Responding reinforces crying; waiting teaches self-soothing.
- **Transactional model**: Infant temperament and parent response co-evolve over time.

## 6. Mechanism
- Stress physiology (cortisol, vagal tone).
- Caregiver sensitivity and contingency.
- Sleep architecture and circadian maturation.

## 7. Evidence
- RCTs of sleep-training methods show no attachment harm through age 6.
- Observational links between responsive care and later regulation (attenuated in sibling designs).
- Certainty grade: **Moderate** for short-term sleep outcomes; **Low** for long-term regulation.

## 8. Boundary
- Evidence does not establish that one approach "creates" self-regulation.
- Temperament and family context moderate all effects.

## 9. System
- Connects to attachment, executive function, and later emotional development.
- Illustrates the Atlas Principle: mapping boundaries between regulation and autonomy.

## 10. Return
The infant has not changed. The reader now sees the cry not as a demand but as a regulatory signal in a developing system.
