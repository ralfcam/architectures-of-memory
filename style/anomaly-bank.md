# Multi-Series Anomaly Bank (Chapter Opening Hooks)

Chapters in both series open with a concrete puzzle, anomaly, or historical paradox where naive intuition fails.

---

## Series 1: *Architectures of Memory*
See individual book outlines under [`series/architectures-of-memory/`](../series/architectures-of-memory/):
- **Book 1 (*The Vanishing Signal*):**
  - *Ch 01 (Perceptron):* Frank Rosenblatt's 1958 Navy press conference vs. the single-layer linear separability wall (`XOR`).
  - *Ch 07–08 (Vanishing Gradient):* Sepp Hochreiter's 1991 diploma thesis showing that backpropagation through time multiplies error signals by Jacobian eigenvalues until the past literally vanishes ($|\lambda| < 1$) or explodes ($|\lambda| > 1$).
  - *Ch 09–11 (Organizational Rot):* Why organizations with more documentation and compliance checklists often lose operational memory faster across reorganizations.
- **Book 2 (*The Forget Gate*):** Why a neural network could only learn to remember once Felix Gers added a mechanism explicitly designed to *forget* (2000).
- **Book 3 (*The Weight of Attention*):** How Bahdanau et al. (2015) and Vaswani et al. (2017) bypassed sequential compression by keeping every token simultaneous—at an $O(N^2)$ quadratic cost.

## Series 2: *Parenthood: The Atlas*
Full domain-by-domain developmental anomaly bank: [`series/parenthood-atlas/style/anomaly-bank.md`](../series/parenthood-atlas/style/anomaly-bank.md).
- *Ch 01 (Preconception):* Parental exposures before conception correlate with offspring traits, yet sibling and genetically informed designs attenuate most headline claims.
- *Ch 02 (In Utero):* A newborn recognizes a maternal rhythm heard through amniotic fluid, while routine maternal work stress rarely leaves the deterministic scar popular culture fears.
- *Ch 05 (Attachment):* In Ainsworth's Strange Situation, securely attached infants often cry when the caregiver leaves—and some infants who appear calm on the surface show elevated physiological cortisol.
